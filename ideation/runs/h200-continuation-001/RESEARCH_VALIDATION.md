## Prospective research-quality evaluation

Freeze a held-out set before the next comparison. Include applicable and
inapplicable changes, already-present mechanisms, negative results, misleading
kernel-only wins and ideas with expensive integration. Give selectors the same
baseline, sources, compute/time budget and number of pilot slots.

Compare the history-aware process with a simple keyword-ranked selector and,
if useful, a seeded topic-matched random selector. Random selection is a process
baseline, not a novelty definition. Score candidates without revealing their
selector: factual/source correctness, active-path plausibility, coverage of prior
outcomes, discriminating tests, honest cost/uncertainty and avoidable expensive
attempts. Preserve zero-selection outcomes and all errors.

After pilots, evaluate calibration: predicted versus observed component effects,
engineering effort, GPU-hours and which assumptions failed. After authorized
matched service experiments, compare qualified improvements per total research
and evaluation cost. Neither the current retrospective checks nor one selected
idea can establish that comparative claim.

The next useful result may be a justified hold. In particular, source-correct AV
accounting that never saves material exposed time should be stopped before a
service attempt, just as a fast FP32 router kernel should be held if the deployed
gate uses BF16 weights.
