# Literature mechanisms and transfer limits

Six full texts were fetched and their methods, comparison conditions and limitations used for the following synthesis. The four initial papers fed the focused evidence stages; the two citation follow-ups below inform this root review only. OpenAlex searches returned 20 bounded metadata hits across four queries, not an exhaustive bibliography. Abstract matches are discovery, not proof of implementation or H200 applicability.

| Primary paper | Mechanism relevant here | What transfers into the experiment design | What does not transfer |
|---|---|---|---|
| [BASS, 2404.15778](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.15778) | Ragged batched speculative decoding and attention organization; draft length interacts with batch behavior | H04 distinguishes useful rows from padding and measures draft/verify cost alongside accepted tokens | A100, small dense models and different draft/kernel design do not predict DSpark/EP4 speedup; this paper does not implement the installed request-count fix |
| [CacheRoute, 2608.19677](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.19677) | Prefix-aware routing balances reuse against queue/load imbalance | H09 requires final-rank locality, cache-hit provenance, hot-prefix negative controls and a simple hash/load baseline | A routing policy is not a drop-in shared-state implementation across our two API workers; cache hit rate alone is not service value |
| [Speculating Experts, 2603.19289](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.19289) | Predicting/prefetching experts trades estimator/cache overhead against expert availability | Measure resident/static comparators and total memory/critical-path effects before proposing speculative movement | The present setup has GPU-resident experts and host Engram tables; expert-offload benefits do not establish Engram or MoE dispatch benefit. No trained predictor is proposed here |
| [MISA, 2605.07363](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.07363) | Select a subset of indexer heads and optionally re-rank candidates | Motivates an independent quality-sensitive sparse-indexer research lane and dense/full-head comparator | H200 kernel evidence on V3.2/GLM does not establish quality preservation for V4.1. Head pruning changes scoring; it is not the lossless implementation comparison in H06 |
| [Synergy of Speculative Decoding and Batching, 2310.18813](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.18813) | Optimal speculative length changes with batch size and hardware; analytical cost model | H04 keeps k5 fixed to isolate padding. A future policy must first beat the strongest fixed-k control across batch/context bands | OPT/Llama results on RTX/A100 do not justify enabling adaptive verification on this running model or changing k during a padding comparison |
| [MagicDec, 2408.11049](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11049) | Long-context KV traffic can make speculative decoding worthwhile even at high batch; sparse draft KV reduces draft cost | Use context as well as batch size in held-out conditions, and measure draft/verification/target costs separately | Llama self-speculation and sparse draft KV are not the current DSpark implementation. Its long-context argument is a counterexample to a universal “large batch makes speculation bad” rule, not an H200 result |

The verified citation traversal is **BASS → Synergy**, followed by **Synergy ← MagicDec**. Both edges are present in the fetched primary full text and recorded in [verified-citation-path.json](discovery/verified-citation-path.json). A search engine suggested a direct BASS/MagicDec relationship, but the downloaded MagicDec text did not establish that edge; it is not recorded. The initial OpenAlex empty edge results remain available and are not described as successful traversal.

The literature changed test design and exclusions rather than supplying headline multipliers. Source claims, implementation facts and root transfer predictions remain separate in the experiment cards. No new paper-inspired mechanism is labeled scientifically novel or experimentally validated.
