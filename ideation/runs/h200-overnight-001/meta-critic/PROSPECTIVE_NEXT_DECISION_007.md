## Value/cost test for the next step

First distinguish **technical gain** from **practical service gain**. A native
effect below 2% can be real and worth keeping. The inherited Mooncake service
confirmation claim still requires its 2% lower ITL-gain bound and 3% tail guards.
The new native endpoint did not change that service contract.

For scale only, the approximately 35.9 ms historical primary ITL makes 2% about
718 microseconds per output token. The component saving is already for all five
draft steps; do not multiply it by five or eight GPUs. Under an illustrative
single exposed call per output token, its direct contribution is about
0.13–0.23%. Actual invocation/progress/exposure and queueing can change this
scenario. Historical waiting counters and high GPU-active percentages have not
established queueing amplification. Do not assume amplification to justify a
large benchmark or impose the additive scenario as a mathematical upper bound.

One unprofiled Mooncake pair needs approximately two 18–26 minute cold starts
plus two roughly 22-minute arrival/warmup windows: **80–96 node-minutes before
additional drain**, or about 10.7–12.8 H200-hours. The earlier research plan's
20-GPU-hour screen envelope is a planning ceiling, not an automatic reservation.
Five-pair confirmation is substantially more expensive. Two full hypothesis
attempts remaining is a limit, not a requirement to spend both.

A scoped actual-request native drafting-stage/exposure measurement is attractive
only if it is materially cheaper and resolves a live uncertainty: ordinary
expert/attention execution, valid progress, actual call counts or exposed
absolute savings. Reuse the proven same-stream event mechanism where practical;
do not revive the failing cross-clock trace attribution merely for continuity.
Name the endpoint before outputs and preserve independent correctness. If that
measurement requires an equally expensive new integration framework or pair of
starts with little decision value, skip it rather than add another gate.

One full service screen can be reasonable when lifecycle acceptance is complete,
native activation is established, the expected effect is worth discriminating,
and the screen has an explicit budget and decision consequence. Keep the frozen
screen/promotion rules or register a genuinely different service objective
**before** service data, with its own practical rationale. Do not choose
throughput, a workload, or a lower threshold after discovering the original
endpoint is unfavorable. A single favorable point estimate is screening
evidence, not confirmed service improvement; request/rank counts cannot replace
independent full runs.

If the best estimate remains a very small ITL effect that one screen is unlikely
to resolve, preserving a correct reusable improvement is preferable to buying
an ambiguous service result solely to claim the system succeeded. Record the
research achievement accurately: the pipeline found, adapted and independently
validated an upstream-derived component improvement on this installation. It
has not yet proved improved primary service ITL or invented a novel algorithm.
Continue the portfolio using newly informed expected benefit, engineering time
and measurement cost rather than relabeling the outcome as the larger goal.
